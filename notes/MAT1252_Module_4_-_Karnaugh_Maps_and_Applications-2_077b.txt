Karnaugh maps 

• A Karnaugh map is another way of representing 

Boolean expressions.

• A Karnaugh map (K-map) contains the same 

information as a truth table.  

• Karnaugh maps are used to simplify Boolean 

expressions so that they can be implemented in 
circuits using the smallest number of logic gates.
• Karnaugh maps are arranged as a grid, which 
makes it easier to spot patterns in expressions.
• We will consider 3-variable and 4-variable Karnaugh 

maps.

Minimal sum of products 

Our aim is to find the simplest possible expression that is 
still a sum of products.  

We want a sum of products with the fewest terms and with 
the fewest variables.  

Such an expression is called a minimal sum of products 
(Min SOP), and the corresponding circuit is called a minimal 
AND-OR circuit.

26

Minimal sum of products 

For example, the expression

xyz  +  xyz'  +  xy'z

is a sum of products, and it can be simplified via

xyz +  xyz'  +  xy'z =  xyz +  xyz'  +  xy'z +  xyz

=  xy(z  +  z')  +  xz(y  +  y') 

= xy +  xz

And xy +  xz is a Min SOP.

Notice that we will not simplify further to x(y + z) because this is no
longer a SOP.

27

SOPs,  CSOPs  and  Min SOPs 

A SOP is simply an expression consisting of products of 
variables (with or without complements) added together.  

A CSOP is an expression that has been expanded into its 
fundamental  products. (Uniquely defined.)

A Min SOP is an expression that has been simplified so as 
to consist of the fewest possible terms and variables, 
and still remains a SOP.  (Not uniquely defined.)

So, at one end of the spectrum we have a CSOP, and at the 
other end we have a Min SOP. 

28

SOPs,  CSOPs  and  Min SOPs 

For example, consider the expression wyz + w'yz + w'xyz'.

(i)  We can expand it to

w(x+x')yz + w'(x+x')yz + w'xyz'

= wxyz + wx'yz + w'xyz + w'x'yz + w'xyz'  

(CSOP)

(ii)  Or we can simplify it to

(w+w)yz + w'xyz = yz + wxyz

= (1+wx)yz + wxyz = yz + wxyz + wxyz = yz + (wxy)(z+z) 

= yz + w'xy

(Min SOP)

Given a K-map we need to be able to find the corresponding Min SOP.
To do this we look for groups of cells that correspond to products with
as few variables as possible.

So remember:

• Look for groups that are  4 x 2,  4 x 1,  2 x 2,  2 x 1

(because products involve only powers of 2).

• Make groups as large as possible

(the larger the group, the simpler the product).

• Overlap groups if possible

(without introducing redundancy).

• Don't forget the "split groups"

(with cells at opposite edges of the K-map).

A NAND gate has two or more inputs. Its output is the negation of the
product of the inputs. So it is the negation of AND.

One reason why a NAND gate is important is that it is a "universal
gate", which means that a logic circuit can be made entirely from
NANDs, as we shall see shortly.

We can construct a circuit entirely from NANDs because we can
construct AND, OR and NOT gates from NANDs, as shown below.

Therefore, in any AND-OR circuit, the AND, OR and NOT gates can be
replaced by appropriate combinations of NANDs.

So a NAND is called a "universal gate".

NOTANDORConverting an AND-OR circuit to NANDs only 

One way to convert an AND-OR circuit to one that uses only NANDs is
to replace each AND, OR and NOT by the equivalent NAND circuit, and
then simplify where possible.

A simpler algebraic method uses de Morgan's laws.

We begin with the sum of products that represents the circuit.

We negate this expression twice and then use de Morgan's law on the
inner negation to obtain an expression in terms of NANDs.

72

Converting an AND-OR circuit to NANDs only 

Example:

Suppose we want to change the sum of products 

L = xy'z + x'y

into an expression in terms of NANDs. We negate the expression
twice, and use de Morgan's law on the inner negation:
L = (xy'z + x'y) = [(xy'z + x'y)']' = [(xy'z)'(x'y)']'
